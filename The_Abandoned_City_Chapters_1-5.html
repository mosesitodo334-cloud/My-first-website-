The Abandoned City

Written by Gentle

Some places are abandoned. Some memories are not.

In a city swallowed by silence, two strangers follow a clock, a forgotten promise, and the fragile hope that love can find its way home.

Chapter One

The City That Forgot the Sun

✦   ✧   ✦

The first thing Elara noticed about the abandoned city was that it still had a heartbeat.

It was not a human heartbeat. It was the slow ticking of a clock somewhere beneath the streets, a sound that travelled through the empty buildings and trembled in the broken windows. Every sixty seconds, it reminded the world that something here had refused to die.

Kael heard it too. He stood beside her at the entrance, his dark coat moving in the cold wind. Beyond him, towers leaned towards one another like exhausted giants. Ivy swallowed balconies. The old railway disappeared beneath weeds. And above it all, the sky burned gold at the edges, as if evening had come to say goodbye.

“People don't come back here,” Elara whispered.

“People don't come back to places that remember them,” Kael replied.

She looked at him. There were questions in his face, but he had spent so many years hiding them that they had become part of his expression.

They had travelled for eleven days to reach this place. Elara carried a small brass key on a ribbon around her neck. It had belonged to her mother, who had vanished when Elara was six. The only clue left behind was a letter with three words written on it: *Find the clock.*

Kael had his own reason for coming. He had once lived here, before the evacuation, before the sirens, before the night everyone called the Last Evening. He never spoke about the people he had left behind.

They crossed the rusted gate. A faded sign hung above them: **WELCOME TO AURELIA — WHERE EVERY HOME HAS A STORY.**

Elara brushed dust from the letters. “Do you think anyone is still here?”

Kael stopped walking.

“No,” he said. Then, after a pause, “But I think someone wants us to believe they are.”

A light appeared in a window on the seventh floor of a building that had no electricity.

It blinked once.

Then twice.

And from somewhere beneath the street, the clock struck thirteen.

Chapter Two

A Photograph in the Dust

✦   ✧   ✦

They found the house at the end of a narrow street, behind a garden where white flowers had grown through the cracks in the pavement. The front door was unlocked. That frightened Elara more than a locked door would have.

Inside, the air smelled of rain and old paper. A family photograph rested on the mantelpiece. The faces had faded, but one little girl remained clear: a child with a ribbon in her hair and a brass key in her hand.

Elara stepped closer.

“That’s me.”

Kael moved beside her. His expression changed so quickly she almost missed it.

“You were here?” he asked.

“My mother never told me.”

On the back of the photograph, someone had written a date and a sentence: *The day we promised not to forget.*

Elara touched the ink. A memory stirred—not a complete memory, but a fragment. A warm kitchen. A woman humming. Someone lifting her onto a chair so she could see the rain outside.

Then a boy's voice: “If you get scared, count the stars.”

Elara turned to Kael.

“You said that to me.”

He looked away. “You were little.”

“You knew my mother?”

“I knew everyone in this house.”

The silence that followed was full of things he would not say.

In the next room, they discovered a child's drawing pinned beneath a fallen picture frame. It showed three people standing beneath a clock tower. One was a woman. One was a boy. The third was a little girl with a bright yellow scarf.

On the back, in careful handwriting, were the words: *When the bells ring, bring her home.*

Elara's fingers tightened around the key.

“Kael, what happened here?”

He took a long breath. “The city was evacuated after the river barriers failed. My little sister was inside the old station. Your mother went back for her.”

“And you?”

“I was supposed to wait.”

His voice broke on the last word.

Outside, the clock began to tick faster.

Chapter Three

The Boy Who Stayed Behind

✦   ✧   ✦

That night, they sheltered in the house. Rain tapped the roof, and the city groaned in the wind.

Kael sat on the floor beside the window, turning a small wooden bird over in his hands. Elara had found it in his coat pocket when he reached for a match. One wing was cracked. Its painted blue eye had almost disappeared.

“Your sister's?” she asked.

He nodded.

“What's her name?”

“Lina.”

He said it as if the name were a room he could still enter.

Kael told her about the day the sirens sounded. He had been twelve. Lina was seven and afraid of thunder. Their mother had gone to help neighbours reach the evacuation buses. Kael had promised to keep Lina safe until she returned.

But the station gates had jammed. The crowd surged. In the confusion, he lost Lina's hand.

He searched until the water reached his waist. He called her name until his voice disappeared. When the rescue workers found him, he was still standing at the station entrance, holding the wooden bird she had dropped.

“I left her,” he said.

“You were a child.”

“I was her brother.”

Elara sat beside him. “You were a child who was frightened and trying to survive. Those things can both be true.”

He stared at the bird. For years, he had treated guilt like a debt that could never be paid. He had mistaken carrying it for keeping his sister close.

Elara showed him the photograph. “My mother went back for her. Maybe she found her.”

“Maybe,” he whispered.

They searched the house again. Behind a loose floorboard, Elara found a folded map marked with a red circle around the clock tower. Beneath it was a note in her mother's handwriting:

*If Elara returns, tell her the truth. The city did not take us. We chose to stay until the last child was safe.*

Kael read it twice.

Then he sat down, as though the words had taken the strength from his knees.

From the street came a sound like a little girl laughing.

Kael rose so quickly the wooden bird fell from his hands.

“Lina?”

Chapter Four

Letters Beneath the Clock

✦   ✧   ✦

The clock tower stood in the centre of Aurelia, surrounded by a square where trees had grown through the old stones. Its great hands were frozen at 11:47.

The brass key fit a narrow door at the base.

Inside, a spiral staircase descended instead of rising. The air grew warmer as they went down. At the bottom was a room filled with shelves, letters, and hundreds of small glass jars. Each jar held a folded piece of paper.

Elara opened the nearest letter.

*To the person who finds this: I hope you found somewhere safe. I hope someone remembered to tell you that you mattered.*

She opened another.

*Dear Mama, I am not afraid of the dark anymore. I am only afraid you will forget my voice.*

The letters were from the people who had remained in Aurelia during the evacuation. They had written to the people they loved, leaving messages in the clock room in case anyone ever returned.

At the centre of the room lay a thick envelope bearing Elara's name.

Her hands trembled as she opened it.

Her mother's handwriting filled the page.

*My little star, if you are reading this, then you have grown beyond the years I was allowed to witness. I am sorry for every birthday I missed, every question you had to carry alone, and every night you wondered whether I had forgotten you.*

Elara pressed the letter to her chest.

*I did not leave because loving you became difficult. I left because the city was falling, and there were children who could not reach the buses without help. I believed I would return before you were old enough to remember the waiting. I was wrong about the time. I was never wrong about loving you.*

The letter ended with a final line:

*Look behind the thirteenth bell.*

Elara lowered the page. Tears blurred the room.

Kael stood a few steps away, staring at another envelope. It was addressed to him.

He did not open it.

“Why?” Elara asked gently.

“Because if it's from Lina, I don't know if I can bear what it says.”

Elara reached for his hand. “You don't have to be brave all at once.”

For a long moment, he held on.

Then, from above them, the clock struck once.

Twice.

By the thirteenth bell, the entire tower was trembling.

Chapter Five

The Last Light in Aurelia

✦   ✧   ✦

Behind the clock's great mechanism was a narrow compartment. Inside lay a lantern, a child's scarf, and a small audio recorder wrapped in cloth.

The scarf was yellow.

Elara recognised it from the drawing.

Kael lifted the recorder with both hands. A label had been tied around it: *For the boy who kept waiting.*

His thumb hovered over the button. Elara stood beside him, close enough that he could hear her breathing.

A woman's voice filled the room.

“Kael, if you have found this, then I hope you have finally forgiven yourself enough to listen.”

He closed his eyes.

“Lina made it to the shelter. She was frightened, but she was alive. Your mother found her near the station, and I helped them reach the old school. We stayed because there were children still waiting for their families. I promised I would tell you.”

A pause. A soft rustle.

“Your sister asked me to give you something. She said you would understand.”

There was a small sound, then a child's voice.

“Kael? It's Lina. I kept the bird. Well, I kept one wing. You can have it back when you come home. I know you're scared, but I'm not angry. I just want you to stop standing in the rain.”

The recording ended.

Kael did not move.

For years, he had imagined a thousand endings. In every one, he was the reason his sister was gone. He had never imagined that she had been waiting somewhere, alive, asking him to come home.

Elara placed the broken wooden bird in his palm. He had carried it all this way without knowing why.

A final note was tucked beneath the recorder. It held an address outside the city and a date written in faded ink. The date was recent—only three years old.

“She might still be there,” Elara whispered.

Kael looked at the address. His eyes were wet, but for the first time since Elara had met him, his face held something other than regret.

Hope frightened him. It asked him to move.

The clock began ticking again, steady and clear. Somewhere beyond the tower, a light appeared in a distant window.

Elara unfolded the last page of her mother's letter. Beneath the farewell was a sentence she had not noticed before:

*If you find the light, do not follow it alone.*

She looked at Kael.

He held out his hand.

Together, they climbed towards the morning.

Aurelia was still abandoned. Its streets were still broken, its windows still empty. But the city no longer felt like a graveyard of forgotten people.

It felt like a letter that had finally reached the right hands.

And beyond its gates, two lives were waiting to begin again.
